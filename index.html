Kodama

A trusted friend who carries your story.

Kodama is an identity continuity service. It allows donors, after they die or retire, to pass their knowledge and decision-making capabilities to future designated heirs — through a living AI guide built from their personal memories, experiences and judgement decisions.

Why Kodama

When someone goes,
their judgement goes with them.

We inherit houses, companies and photo albums. We rarely inherit what made them work: how a person thought, what they weighed, and why they chose as they did.

一

It leaves all at once

A founder retires, a parent dies, a mentor steps back. Decades of context — who to trust, what was tried, what really mattered — leave in a single day.

二

Archives can't answer back

Letters, videos and documents keep what was said. They can't answer the question you have today, about a situation they never saw.

三

Heirs are left guessing

Without that context, the people who carry on are left to guess what they would have done — and often to disagree about it.

What Kodama is

A living guide,
built from a life.

While they are alive, the donor tells Kodama their story: the memories, the experiences, and the decisions they made — and why. Kodama turns it into a guide the people they choose can talk to.

It is not an impersonation. Kodama never pretends to be the person; it speaks about them, like a trusted friend who knew them well, and only from what they actually said.

How it works

Two conversations, one memory.

Everything happens by voice. One person speaks to be remembered; others speak to remember them.

The donor

Kodama listens

Guided voice interviews, at the donor's own pace. Kodama asks what a good friend would — about people, turning points, and the hard calls — so nothing important is left to chance.

  • Memories, experiences and the reasoning behind decisions
  • A self-portrait of values and temperament
  • The donor reviews everything Kodama keeps
their story

The heirs

Kodama remembers

A child, a successor, an old friend — someone the donor named — sits down and asks. Kodama answers from what the donor actually said, and tells them where it came from.

  • Only the heirs the donor names, each with their own key
  • Every claim about the donor is cited — or Kodama abstains
  • Each heir's conversations stay private to them
  1. I

    Capture

    The donor speaks, while alive. Their words are kept as they said them.

  2. II

    Compile

    Kodama distils people, events, values and decisions — and what is unknown.

  3. III

    Converse

    After they die or retire, their heirs talk with Kodama. It cites, or it stays quiet.

  4. IV

    Rest

    How a guide eventually comes to rest is designed in from the start.

Grounded

Cite, or stay silent.

A language model knows a great deal about the world. It knows nothing about your grandmother.

So Kodama keeps the two apart. Every claim about the donor's life points back to their own words. When there is nothing to point to, Kodama says so. Warmth is welcome; invention is not.

Promises

Six things we will not bend.

一

Lifetime consent

Only a living adult can donate, and only for themselves. Consent is explicit, revisited, and theirs to withdraw.

二

No advertising, ever

A person's memory is not inventory, and a family's attention is not the product.

三

Never an impersonation

Kodama speaks about the donor, never as them. It is their friend — not their replacement.

四

No therapeutic claims

Kodama is a guide, not a treatment. A named clinical advisory board keeps us honest about that line.

五

The right to refuse

Heirs are invited, never enrolled. Anyone may decline to listen, and each conversation stays theirs alone.

六

Designed retirement

How a guide eventually comes to rest is designed in from the start — not left to chance.
